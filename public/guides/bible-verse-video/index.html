How to Add a Moving Background to a Bible Verse Video

A verse on a still picture is easy to scroll past. A gentle moving background, like soft light rays, drifting clouds or a calm gradient, makes people pause and read. Here's how to make a verse video that looks peaceful and is easy to read.

Choose a background that fits the verse

  • Light rays and clouds for verses about hope, light, heaven and God's presence.
  • Sunsets for evening devotionals, rest and peace.
  • Starry skies for verses about creation, or about God knowing each of us.
  • Soft gradients for any verse, when you want the words to carry everything.

Choose calm, slow motion. Fast or busy backgrounds pull attention away from the words.

Step by step

  1. Set up your project. Use 9:16 for TikTok, Reels and Shorts, or 16:9 for YouTube and church screens.
  2. Add the background as the bottom layer and zoom it to fill the frame.
  3. Type the verse. Break it into short lines of 4 to 7 words, the way you'd read it aloud. For example:
    This is the day / which the LORD hath made; / we will rejoice / and be glad in it.
  4. Add the reference underneath in a smaller font, for example Psalm 118:24 (KJV).
  5. Pick readable fonts. A classic serif for the verse feels timeless; a clean sans-serif for the reference. Use white or cream text with a soft shadow.
  6. Time it. Give viewers enough time: about 1 second for every 3 words, plus 2 seconds. A 20-word verse needs around 9 seconds.
  7. Animate gently. A slow fade-in of each line works better than bouncing or spinning text.
  8. Export at 1080p. If you need a longer video, repeat the background. All our clips loop seamlessly.

Layout tips

  • Leave space around the words. Keep the text inside the middle 80% of the screen.
  • Mind the buttons. On TikTok and Reels, the right side and the bottom are covered by buttons and captions, so keep text a little higher and away from the right edge.
  • Darken if needed. If a bright background makes text hard to read, add a black layer at 20 to 30% opacity between the background and the text.
  • One verse per video is easier to remember and share than several at once.

A note on Bible translations

Older translations such as the King James Version (KJV) are in the public domain in most countries, so you can use them freely. Many modern translations are copyrighted, but most publishers allow short quotations in videos and social posts, as long as you include the translation's name. Check your translation's "permissions" page if you plan to quote a lot, or to sell the video.

For church services

The same backgrounds work behind song lyrics and announcements on a projector or LED wall. See our guide to backgrounds for church LED walls and slideshows.

More ideas

  • A weekly "Verse of the Week" series with the same background, so followers recognise it.
  • A morning devotional: verse first, then a short reflection in your own voice.
  • Quote and "did you know" videos work the same way. Try a gradient background.
