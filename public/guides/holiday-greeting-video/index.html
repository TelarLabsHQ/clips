How to Make a Holiday Greeting Video with a Free Moving Background

A moving background turns a few lines of text into a greeting that feels finished. In this guide we build three common videos, a family Christmas greeting, a concert invitation and a YouTube end screen, from the same free clips, plus a vertical version for TikTok and Reels.

See the finished examples

Here is what we’re making. Every layer comes from free clips on this site and plain text.

How the layers work

Almost every greeting video is three layers stacked on top of each other:

LayerWhat it isSetting
BottomThe moving background, such as northern lights, an aurora gradient or a snow globeNormal. Make it as long as your video.
Middle (optional)An overlay made on black, such as the frost frame, gold dust or light leaksBlend mode: Screen. The black disappears and only the light shows.
TopYour text, logo or photoA short fade-in and a soft shadow so it’s easy to read

Step by step in CapCut

  1. Download your background. For a winter greeting, try Northern Lights over Snowy Mountains or the Snow Globe. For TikTok or Reels, download the Vertical 9:16 version so nothing gets cropped.
  2. Start a new project and add the background clip. It becomes the main track.
  3. Add the overlay (optional). Tap Overlay → Add overlay and pick the Frosty Window Frame. Stretch it to fill the screen, then tap Blend → Screen. The black disappears and the frost sits around the edges.
  4. Add your text. Tap Text → Add text. Put the main line, like Merry Christmas, in a large script or serif font, and the smaller lines underneath in a simple font.
  5. Make it readable. Use white or cream text and turn on a soft shadow. If the background is busy behind small text, lower the background’s brightness a little (Adjust → Brightness).
  6. Animate the text. Choose an in-animation such as Fade in, starting about half a second after the video begins.
  7. Set the length. 8 to 15 seconds is right for most greetings. Our clips loop seamlessly, so copy the background and paste it end to end if you need more time; you won’t see the join.
  8. Export at 1080p.

The same steps in Canva or other editors

  • Canva: upload the clip and use it as the page background. Add an overlay clip on top, then set its Transparency or blend option if your plan has it. If not, choose backgrounds that don’t need an overlay, like the northern lights or snow globe.
  • Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve or iMovie: put the background on the lowest track, the overlay above it with Screen blend mode, and text on top.
  • PowerPoint or Google Slides: insert the clip as a video on a slide, set it to play automatically and loop, and type your text on top. Handy for church screens and classroom displays.

Where to put the text

  • Use the empty space. The northern lights clip has open sky at the top, a natural spot for titles. The snow globe works best with text above or below the globe.
  • Keep vertical videos clear of the edges. TikTok and Reels cover the bottom and right side with buttons and captions, so keep important text in the upper two thirds and away from the right edge.
  • One main line. A greeting reads best with one big line and one or two small ones. Long paragraphs are hard to read in a few seconds.

Wording ideas

  • Merry Christmas & a Happy New Year. With love, the Chen family
  • Season’s Greetings from all of us at Maple Street Bakery
  • You’re invited: Winter Concert, Saturday, December 20, 7:00 PM
  • Thank you for a wonderful year. See you in 2027!
  • Thanks for watching. New videos every Friday

More ideas with the same clips

  • Event invitations: concerts, parties and church programs. Add the date, time and place in the small lines.
  • YouTube intros and end screens: a calm moving background behind your channel name or a Subscribe button.
  • Livestream screens: see our guide to making a “starting soon” screen.
  • Church and classroom screens: see backgrounds for church LED walls.
  • Countdowns: for New Year’s Eve, use the New Year countdown, also available as a vertical version.

A note about music

Music makes a greeting feel complete, but not every song is free to use. If you post publicly, use the music library inside TikTok, Instagram or CapCut, or a track marked free to use. For ads, use your platform’s commercial music library. For a private message to family, any song you like is usually fine.
